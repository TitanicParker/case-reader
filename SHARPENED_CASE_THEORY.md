# Sharpened Case Theory

## Core proposition

This case should be framed as an **ordering breach**, not a diagnostic hindsight complaint.

The central proposition is:

> The record did not merely contain uncertainty. It documented uncertainty while simultaneously converting one hypothesis into administrative fact.

That is the strongest point.

## 1. The referral created an open diagnostic problem

The referral did not ask neurology to confirm an already-settled diagnosis. It referred a deteriorating patient with gait failure, foot-centred symptoms, neurological signs, and occupational decline.

The referral preserved diagnostic openness. The hospital therefore inherited an **open diagnostic problem**, not a settled label.

## 2. The discharge summary declared finality while preserving facts that made finality unsafe

The discharge summary is the central artefact because it performs two inconsistent functions.

At the administrative level, it declares a final primary diagnosis.

At the evidential level, it records features that should have kept the case provisional: atypicality, unresolved foot-centred symptoms, dystonic-looking gait, limited tremor, brisk reflexes, possible genetic disease, broad differential possibilities, and deferred dopaminergic discrimination.

The case in one sentence:

> The discharge summary records the reasons finality was unsafe, then declares finality as if those reasons had been resolved.

## 3. Certification hardened the problem administratively

The statutory certification is not just paperwork. It is the administrative conversion point.

It changes the status of the label. Before certification, the diagnosis could still remain a working hypothesis. After certification, it becomes a reliance-creating administrative fact.

The sharpened theory is therefore:

> The breach was not suspecting Parkinsonism. The breach was certifying Parkinson’s Disease before the record had earned finality.

## 4. The levodopa issue should remain narrow

Do not argue that levodopa had to be started immediately.

The better argument is:

> If levodopa was withheld for therapeutic caution, then diagnostic certainty also had to be withheld.

Therapeutic caution may be clinically defensible. But once a material dopaminergic discriminator was deliberately deferred, the diagnosis should have remained expressly provisional unless finality was justified by other clearly documented reasoning.

## 5. The feet are the jurisdictional anchor

The feet are not a side issue. They are the entry point and continuity marker of the case.

The referral starts with foot-centred gait difficulty. The discharge summary continues to record foot pathology and dystonic-looking gait. Later records show the feet repeatedly returning as the unresolved problem.

The expert question should not be:

> Was Parkinson’s possible?

It should be:

> Where, before discharge and certification, did the hospital resolve the foot-centred neurological problem it accepted at referral?

If there is no clear answer, then the diagnostic sequence did not complete.

## Final formulation

This was not a failure to predict later developments. It was a failure to preserve provisionality when the contemporaneous record itself required provisionality.

The public-facing core argument should be:

> Parkinsonism may have been a reasonable working impression. But Parkinson’s Disease was not entitled to become administratively final. The record itself prevented that entitlement. It showed an atypical, foot-centred, dystonic-looking, genetically possible, levodopa-undiscriminated case. Yet the system declared, certified, and relied. That is finality before inquiry.
